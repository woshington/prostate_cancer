Nesta seção apresentamos a avaliação quantitativa dos modelos individuais e das abordagens de ensemble, seguindo a metodologia descrita na Seção \ref{sec:materials_methods}.

\subsection{Evolution of the EfficientNet-B0 Base Model}

Starting from a baseline model with a Kappa of 0.826, incremental improvements were applied, resulting in consistent performance gains. The adoption of Ordinal Loss increased the $\kappa_{\text{quad}}$ from 0.838 to 0.851, highlighting its effectiveness in capturing the ordinal structure of the classes and subsequently, combining Focal Loss with entropy-based filtering further improved performance, reaching a $\kappa_{\text{quad}}$ of 0.856 and establishing the best set of hyperparameters for the EfficientNet-B0 architecture, outperforming the baseline by 3.0 percentage points. Figure~\ref{fig:loss_kappa_focal} shows that the validation loss remained stable, while the $\kappa_{\text{quad}}$ steadily improved up to approximately 0.860, suggesting consistent model improvement without significant signs of overfitting.

\subsection{Impact of Model Depth}

We applied the same pipeline structure (learning rate, number of epochs, optimizer, and loss functions) to deeper architectures (EfficientNet-B3 and B7). Contrary to initial expectations, the obtained results were 0.848 and 0.844, respectively, both lower than that achieved by EfficientNet-B0. This observation suggests that simply increasing model capacity, without architecture-specific hyperparameter adjustments or appropriate regularization strategies, not only failed to yield benefits but also led to slight performance degradation. This behavior indicates a higher susceptibility to overfitting or the need for a more refined optimization process for deeper architectures.

\subsection{Performance of Ensemble Strategies}

Table \ref{tab:ensemble_results_expanded} presents a comparison of the methods, including QWK, accuracy, standard deviations, and 95\% confidence intervals. Consistently, the ensemble strategies outperformed all individual models. The simple averaging method achieved the best performance, with QWK = 0.879 and an accuracy of 69.8\%, representing a gain of 2.3 percentage points over the best individual model (B0-Entropy-Ordinal-Focal). This result indicates that combining predictions leverages complementarities across different architectures and enhances their robustness.

The weighted averaging ensemble achieved virtually equivalent performance (QWK = 0.878) to simple averaging, with only a marginal difference, suggesting that weight assignment did not yield a significant gain under the evaluated conditions, possibly due to the need for more refined calibration. The majority voting method performed poorly (QWK = 0.859), though it still outperformed individual models, suggesting that strategies based on discrete aggregation are less effective than probability-based combinations. The maximum-based method exhibited the worst performance (QWK = 0.839), even falling below several individual models, suggesting that selecting the most confident prediction tends to amplify uncertainties and introduce instability into the decision process.

In terms of accuracy, the same pattern was observed: the simple averaging ensemble reached 69.8\%, while individual models ranged between 60.2\% and 66.0\%. These results reinforce that model aggregation improves not only ordinal agreement (QWK) but also the rate of correct classifications, highlighting the positive impact of ensemble strategies on the stability and generalization of the system.
